How Dialbubble works

Dialbubble is a remote control for your Twilio account. You tap, the app asks Twilio to do it, and Twilio places the call or sends the text.

Setting up

The app asks if you already have a Twilio account and helps you sign up if you don't. Then it walks you through this, one screen at a time. If you leave partway, it picks up where you stopped.

  1. 1

    Find your keys

    Open the Twilio Console and copy your Account SID and Auth Token. You can create an API Key instead. The app shows you exactly where to look, and Smart Paste fills the right fields from your clipboard.
  2. 2

    Connect

    Dialbubble checks your credentials with Twilio and loads your account. If something fails, it tells you why and how to fix it.
  3. 3

    Your numbers

    Choose which of your Twilio numbers to use, and pick defaults for calling and texting. If you don't have a number yet, the app links you to where you can buy or verify one.
  4. 4

    Your phone

    Enter your own phone number. This is the phone Twilio rings for two-way Talk calls.
  5. 5

    Try it now

    Dialbubble sets up your first text or call, so you can see it work straight away.

To schedule texts, add a Messaging Service SID later under Settings, Account. Want to look around first? Tap Explore Demo on the welcome screen and skip all of this.

How a two-way call works

Dialbubble has no VoIP, because in-app calling needs a server. Instead, Twilio connects you on your regular phone.

  1. 1

    Twilio rings you

    You start the call in Dialbubble. Twilio calls the phone number you entered during setup.
  2. 2

    You press any key

    A voice names the last digits of the number you're calling and asks you to press a key. If no key is pressed, the call is cancelled and the other person is never dialed.
  3. 3

    Twilio dials them

    The other person's phone rings, showing your Twilio number.
  4. 4

    You talk

    You're on a normal call on your phone. Dialbubble shows the status and can hang up.

Two legs, two charges

Twilio bills the call to you and the call to them separately, at its normal rates. If you record the call, Twilio charges for that as well.

Other kinds of call

Only Talk rings you. The others call the other person directly and don't need you on the line. All of them can use your Twilio number as the caller ID.

Talk
Two-way. Twilio rings your phone, you press any key, and Twilio dials the other person and joins you. Bills two legs.
Voice Message
Twilio calls the other person and reads your text aloud with text-to-speech. Bills one leg.
Play Audio
Twilio calls the other person and plays an MP3 or WAV from a public link. Bills one leg.
Custom TwiML
Twilio calls the other person and runs the TwiML you wrote. Whatever it does is billed as usual.

How texting works

Messages go out from your Twilio number through Twilio. The app reads your message history back from Twilio and arranges it into conversations.

  • While the app is open, it checks for new messages and calls every 20 seconds, and every 5 seconds inside an open conversation.
  • While it's closed, it can check now and then through Background App Refresh and show an alert, if you turn that on. iOS decides when those checks run, so alerts can be late.
  • To send a picture, you give Dialbubble a public https link and Twilio fetches the media from there. The app has no server to upload to.
  • Scheduled texts are held by Twilio, so they go out even when your phone is off. They need a Twilio Messaging Service.
  • US texting has its own registration rules (A2P 10DLC) at Twilio, and the in-app help explains them.

The limits, plainly

No in-app calling
Dialbubble doesn't do VoIP. A Talk call rings your regular phone through a bridge, so it bills two Twilio legs.
No instant push
The app checks for new texts every 20 seconds while it's open. Alerts while it's closed are optional and best effort, because iOS decides when background checks run.
iPhone only
Dialbubble needs iOS 26 or later. There's no Android or iPad version.
A Twilio account for real use
Demo Mode works without one. Real calls and texts run on your own Twilio account.
Incoming calls stay with Twilio
Calls to your Twilio number follow whatever you set up in the Twilio Console. Dialbubble doesn't answer them.
Twilio trial accounts have limits
Trial accounts can only reach verified numbers. Twilio also blocks two-way Talk calls, recording and custom text on them, so Voice Message is the call type that works. Those rules come from Twilio, and upgrading removes them.
