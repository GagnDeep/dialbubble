How to make calls from a different number

To call from a different number, use one you own or have verified: add a second line to your iPhone, use a second-number app, or set a Twilio number or verified number as your caller ID. Showing a number that isn't yours to mislead someone is spoofing, which is illegal in the US and many other places.

What are your options for calling from another number?

Each route below gives you a real second number that people can call back. The right one depends on whether you need a separate line all day or only want to show a certain number now and then.

On an iPhone with Dual SIM, Apple says you can label each plan, such as Business and Personal, and choose which number to use for calls and messages. If that sounds like what you want, our comparison of second business numbers on iPhone sets the costs side by side.

Is it legal to call from a different number?

Using a number you own or have the right to use is fine. The FCC's own example of a legitimate use is a doctor calling from a personal mobile and displaying the office number. What the law targets is deception.

  • United States. The Truth in Caller ID Act, at 47 U.S.C. § 227(e), makes it unlawful to knowingly send misleading or inaccurate caller ID with intent to defraud, cause harm or wrongfully obtain anything of value. The statute sets penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, and the FCC adjusts its figures over time.
  • United Kingdom. Ofcom's caller ID guidance says the number sent with a call must be valid, dialable and uniquely identify the caller, and providers must try to stop calls that fail that test.
  • Twilio's terms. Twilio's Acceptable Use Policy forbids creating a false identity or trying to mislead others about who sent a communication. Twilio also enforces this technically: its API only accepts a Twilio number or a verified number.

Rules differ by country, so check locally if you call abroad. This is general information, not legal advice. Hiding your number is a separate thing: the statute doesn't restrict blocking your own caller ID, and the FCC says doing so isn't spoofing.

How do you call from a Twilio number?

Here's a concrete case. Maya runs a small bakery and wants customers to see her shop's Twilio number, +1 415 555 0199, when she calls from her mobile, +1 415 555 0111. She's calling a customer on +1 415 555 0123. These are the steps.

  1. Get a number you have the right to use. Buy a voice-enabled number in the Twilio Console, or add a verified caller ID for a phone you already own. If you're new to this, how to use a Twilio phone number covers the basics.
  2. Ring your own phone from Twilio. Twilio has no handset of its own, so a two-way call starts with Twilio calling you. Set To to your mobile and From to your Twilio number.
  3. Connect the other person with Dial. The TwiML in the request runs when you pick up. It dials the customer and shows them the number in callerId.
  4. Answer and check what they see. Pick up when your phone rings. Try it on a friend's phone first.

Twilio's Call resource docs say From must be a Twilio number or a verified outgoing caller ID for your account. The Dial docs set a similar limit on callerId. Put your numbers in E.164 format, with a plus sign and country code.

Upgrade first

This won't run on a trial account. Twilio's trial docs list <Dial><Number> as blocked, and replace it with a spoken message. Trials also use shared numbers, so you can't show your own until you upgrade.

Think about call-backs before you settle on a number. If a customer calls +1 415 555 0199 back, that Twilio number does nothing until you tell it what to do. Set up call forwarding on your Twilio number so those calls reach you.

Dialbubble runs this same flow from a button if you'd rather not use a terminal, and adds a safety step: you press a key before the other person is dialed. It has no in-app calling, so your regular phone still rings and you still pay for two legs.

What does it cost to call from a Twilio number?

A US call costs $0.0140 a minute for each leg, and a US local number costs $1.15 a month, as of November 2026 on Twilio's US voice pricing. Maya's call has two legs: Twilio to her, and Twilio to the customer.

Maya's month, worked out

One 6-minute call: 6 minutes × 2 legs × $0.0140 = $0.168, or about 17 cents.

40 calls of 6 minutes: 240 minutes × 2 legs × $0.0140 = $6.72.

Add the number: $6.72 + $1.15 = $7.87 for the month.

If she shows a verified number instead, she isn't renting that number from Twilio, so the $1.15 fee for it falls away, though the call legs cost the same. Try your own numbers in the Twilio pricing calculator.

Why does Twilio refuse my number?

The usual cause is error 21210, "From phone number not verified". It means the number isn't a Twilio number on this account and hasn't been verified on it. Check that you're using the right account, then verify the number or buy one. Don't look for a way around it: that check is what stops people from impersonating numbers they don't control.

If calls go through but show up as "spam likely", the number is working and the label comes from elsewhere. Our guide to how outbound caller ID works explains who decides that and what you can do. Trial accounts have their own limits on who you can call, covered in Twilio's free trial limits.

Questions people ask

Can you call from a different number on an iPhone?
Yes, if the iPhone has a second line. With Dual SIM you can choose which of your two numbers to use for a call. Without a second line, you need an app or service that gives you another number, because iPhone has no setting to display an arbitrary number.
Is it illegal to show a different number when I call?
Showing a number you own or are authorized to use is generally fine. In the US, it's unlawful to knowingly send misleading or inaccurate caller ID with intent to defraud, cause harm or wrongfully obtain something of value. Other countries have their own rules. This is general information, not legal advice.
Can I use Twilio to show someone else's number?
No. Twilio requires the number on an outgoing call to be one you bought from Twilio or verified. Verification means someone answers a call on that number and enters a code, which proves control of it. Don't try to verify a number that isn't yours.
Can I hide my number instead?
Yes. US law doesn't restrict blocking your own caller ID, and the FCC says that isn't spoofing. Many people ignore calls from unknown numbers, though, so a real number you can be called back on often works better.
