A2P 10DLC registration: cost and steps

A2P 10DLC registration tells US carriers who you are and what you text, and it's required for anyone sending SMS from a Twilio local number to the US. As of November 2026, a Sole Proprietor registration costs $19.50 up front plus $2 a month, and campaign review takes up to 5 business days for standard use cases.

This guide explains Twilio's process in plain words. It isn't legal advice. Fees and rules can change, so check the linked Twilio pages before you pay, and ask a lawyer about consent rules for your business.

Do you need to register?

Twilio's A2P 10DLC docs are direct about this: anyone using a local number with Twilio to send SMS to the US needs to register, including individuals and hobbyists. Carriers treat every text sent through Twilio as application-to-person, even one you typed by hand. Without registration, your texts are blocked with error 30034.

You can skip it if one of these fits:

  • You send from a toll-free number. It has its own toll-free verification, and US and Canadian traffic is blocked until it's approved.
  • You send from a short code. As of November 2026 these start at $1,000 a month, so few small senders choose them.
  • You only send login codes. Twilio suggests Verify for that.

Which A2P brand type fits you?

A brand is the legal person or business doing the sending. The two questions that decide your type are whether you have a Tax ID and how much you send. Volumes below come from Twilio's brand docs.

Here is what one campaign adds up to over the first year, before you pay for the number or the texts:

  • Sole Proprietor: $4.50 + $15 + (12 x $2) = $43.50
  • Low-Volume Standard at $1.50 a month: $4.50 + $15 + (12 x $1.50) = $37.50
  • Standard at $10 a month: $4.50 + $41.50 + $15 + (12 x $10) = $181

These fees are on top of per-message and number charges. The A2P 10DLC cost calculator runs the same sums for your setup, and how Twilio pricing works covers the rest of the bill.

How to register, step by step

Start from the A2P onboarding or Trust Hub section of the Twilio Console. The exact menu depends on which version of the Console you're using. Twilio has full walkthroughs for Sole Proprietors and Standard brands.

  1. Choose your brand type. Use the table above. Don't pick Sole Proprietor to save money if you have a Tax ID, because you aren't eligible.
  2. Create your Trust Hub profile. Enter your details exactly as they appear on your tax records. A Primary Customer Profile can take 72 hours or more to approve, but you can move on while it's pending.
  3. Register your brand. Most reviews finish within minutes, while manual ones can take seven business days or more. Sole Proprietors get a one-time passcode by text and must respond within 24 hours. That mobile must be a real US or Canadian one, not a Twilio number, and it can be used for at most three Sole Proprietor brands.
  4. Set up a Messaging Service. Add the number you want to send from to its sender pool. On a Sole Proprietor campaign, keep exactly one number in it, because extra numbers stay unregistered.
  5. Register a campaign. Choose the use case and fill in the details described next. Reviews take up to 5 business days for standard use cases, and some special ones need an extra carrier review.
  6. Send a test once approved. If you see error 30035, the number is still registering. That can take up to 24 hours, and you shouldn't remove and re-add it in the meantime.

  • The consent wording gives the message type, frequency, "Message and data rates may apply" and how to opt out.
  • The samples match the use case and include the business name, so they agree with the description.

Common reasons campaigns get rejected

  • A vague description. "This campaign sends messages to customers" tells a reviewer nothing.
  • A message flow reviewers can't check. Describe each opt-in method separately, and make sure any website URL you give is live and public.
  • Mismatched details. The brand name, website, email domain and samples should all point to the same business.
  • Sharing opt-in data. Your privacy policy should say you don't sell or share it with third parties or lead generators.
  • Generic short links. Twilio warns that services such as bit.ly lead to rejection. Use a branded domain.
  • Forbidden content. Some categories are barred however you word them. Twilio's forbidden message categories include gambling, cannabis, debt relief and third-party lending.

If a campaign fails, edit and resubmit it instead of creating a new one. Twilio's vetting FAQ and campaign troubleshooting guide cover the details. Campaigns rejected for disallowed content generally can't be resubmitted.

What happens after you submit?

You can only send compliant A2P traffic once the campaign is fully approved. If it's still in review after 10 days, Twilio says to contact Support. After approval, watch for these:

  • Rate limits. Error 30022 means you hit the campaign's rate limit. Error 30023 means the daily brand cap, which resets at 00:00 Pacific.
  • Filtering. Registration lowers filtering but doesn't end it. If you still get blocked, work through how to fix error 30007.
  • Opt-outs. Honor every STOP. Texting a person after they opt out produces error 21610.

Questions

Do I have to register if I only send a few texts?
Yes, if you send them from a US local number through Twilio. Twilio says carriers treat all its SMS traffic as application-to-person, and that this includes individuals and hobbyists. There's no minimum volume.
Can I avoid A2P 10DLC?
You can use a different sender. Toll-free numbers and short codes aren't part of A2P 10DLC, but toll-free numbers need their own verification and short codes cost far more. If you only send login codes, Twilio suggests looking at Verify.
Can I register as a Sole Proprietor if my business has an EIN?
No. Twilio says that if you have a US EIN or Canadian Business Number, you aren't eligible for a Sole Proprietor brand and must register as Standard or Low-Volume Standard.
Is the $15 campaign fee refunded if I'm rejected?
No. Twilio's vetting FAQ says it's non-refundable. It also advises editing and resubmitting a failed campaign rather than creating a new one, since the fee is charged per campaign.
Is this guide legal advice?
No. It explains Twilio's registration process in plain words. For questions about what the law requires of your business, such as consent rules, talk to a qualified lawyer.
