GSM-7 vs UCS-2: SMS encoding and segments

GSM-7 fits 160 characters in one SMS segment but only supports a limited alphabet, while UCS-2 supports nearly any character but fits just 70. One emoji or curly quote switches the whole message to UCS-2, which can turn a one-segment text into three and triple what you pay.

GSM-7 vs UCS-2 at a glance

An SMS segment carries 140 bytes. The encoding decides how many characters fit in them. These figures come from Twilio's message length docs.

MeasureGSM-7UCS-2
Size of each character7 bits16 bits
Single-segment limit16070
Per segment in a longer message15367
Per segment, toll-free to US or Canada15266
Covers128 characters, mostly LatinEmoji, Chinese, Arabic, Hindi and much more

You don't choose the encoding yourself. Twilio picks the most compact one that fits, so if any character falls outside GSM-7, the entire message becomes UCS-2, not just that character.

What are GSM characters?

The GSM-7 set has 128 characters: A to Z, a to z, 0 to 9, common punctuation, and a selection of accented letters and Greek capitals. It looks like ASCII, but it's not the same, and that catches people out.

TypeCharactersEffect
Safe, 1 eachA-Z a-z 0-9 . , ! ? : ; - ' " @ £ $ % & and é è ñ üStays GSM-7
Safe, 2 each{ } [ ] \ ~ ^ | and €Stays GSM-7, counts double
Not in the setCurly quotes, long dashes, á í ó ú ç, emoji, non-Latin scriptsWhole message goes to UCS-2

Characters that count double need an escape character first. So Your code is {4821} has 39 visible characters but counts as 41. To test your own text character by character, paste it into the GSM-7 character checker.

Why are the limits 153 and 67, not 160 and 70?

The arithmetic for a single segment is simple:

  • GSM-7: (140 bytes x 8 bits) / 7 bits = 160 characters
  • UCS-2: (140 bytes x 8 bits) / 16 bits = 70 characters

Once a message needs more than one segment, each one carries a 6-byte header telling the phone how to put the parts back together. That leaves 134 bytes, which gives 153 GSM-7 characters or 67 UCS-2 ones.

What does the switch to UCS-2 cost?

As of November 2026, Twilio's US SMS pricing is $0.0083 per segment plus a carrier fee, which is $0.0045 on T-Mobile. That makes $0.0128 per segment. Sending the text above to 1,000 T-Mobile customers:

  • Version A: 1,000 x 1 x $0.0128 = $12.80
  • Version B or C: 1,000 x 3 x $0.0128 = $38.40

That's $25.60 extra for one character. Segments also use up your sending allowance, because rate limits and A2P 10DLC daily caps are counted in segments, not messages. See how Twilio pricing works for the rest of the bill.

How do you stay in GSM-7?

  1. Write templates in a plain text editor. Word processors and some notes apps swap straight quotes for curly ones. Twilio points out that smart quotes and non-standard spaces are a common cause.
  2. Check before a big send. Paste the final text, with sample names filled in, into a checker. A long name or an accented one can push a message over the limit.
  3. Turn on Smart Encoding. If you use a Messaging Service, this Content Settings option replaces lookalikes such as curly quotes with GSM characters. It doesn't change emoji or scripts like Korean.
  4. Read num_segments on a test message. Twilio's API reports the real count after sending. It starts at 0 on a Messaging Service, since no sender has been picked yet, so check it again afterwards.
  5. Keep it short anyway. Twilio recommends staying under 320 characters, and the body limit is 1,600 (error 21617).

UCS-2 isn't a mistake

If your customers read Arabic, Hindi, Chinese or Vietnamese, UCS-2 is the right encoding and worth paying for. Write in their language and budget for the extra segments. The aim is to avoid paying for characters you never meant to use.

Questions

How many segments does a 161-character text use?
Two, if it's all GSM-7. Twilio's example splits it into one segment of 153 characters and one of 8.
Why did my message switch to UCS-2 when I can't see anything unusual?
A word processor or phone keyboard has probably added a curly quote, a long dash or a non-standard space. They look normal on screen, but they aren't in GSM-7, so the whole message switches.
Are accented letters safe in GSM-7?
Some are. é, è, ù, ì, ò, à, ñ, ä, ö, ü and Ç are in the set, but á, í, ó, ú and ç aren't. One of those will switch the message to UCS-2.
Does the encoding change how much I pay for each segment?
No. The price per segment is the same. UCS-2 costs more because the same text splits into more segments.
Does MMS or RCS use these limits?
MMS doesn't use SMS segments. RCS uses UTF-8 and handles emoji natively, though Twilio says US delivery is still billed per segment of 160 UTF-8 characters.
