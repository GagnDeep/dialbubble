Second phone number for business on iPhone

You can add a second business number to your iPhone with a second carrier line on eSIM, a hosted business phone app, or your own Twilio account. An eSIM or hosted app is the easier fit for most people, while Twilio suits low or irregular use if you don't mind a more technical setup.

Can you add a second number with eSIM?

Yes, and it's the most natural option on iPhone. Per Apple's Dual SIM guide, you need an iPhone XS, XR or later and a carrier that supports eSIM. If the two plans come from different carriers, your iPhone must be unlocked. iPhone 13 and later can run two eSIMs at once.

  1. Ask your carrier, or another carrier, for a second plan with an eSIM.
  2. Activate it from the iPhone's cellular settings.
  3. Label the plans, such as Business and Personal. You'll use those labels to pick a number for calls and messages.
  4. Assign the right number to each contact, so calling them back uses the right line.

Both numbers can make and receive calls and send iMessage, SMS or MMS, with only one using cellular data at a time. It's very simple day to day, and calls come in the normal way with your normal voicemail. Its limits only show up if you want to share the line with a colleague or manage it from a computer.

Is a hosted business phone app better?

For many small businesses, it is. A hosted service gives you the number and its calling and texting tools ready to go, with extras that depend on the plan. You don't need to set up anything technical.

  • Google Voice has paid plans at $10, $20 and $30 per user per month, and a basic no-cost plan. Calls to the US and Canada are generally free when you call from the US. The paid one-user Starter plan is US only. See our Dialbubble and Google Voice comparison.
  • Quo, formerly OpenPhone, charges $15, $23 or $35 per user per month billed yearly, or $19, $33 or $47 monthly, with a 7-day trial. Extra numbers are $5 a month. Texting needs carrier registration, with a $19.50 one-time fee and $1.50 to $3 a month. See Dialbubble and Quo side by side.
  • Grasshopper starts at $14 a month and serves only the US and Canada. See how Grasshopper compares.

Hosted apps beat a Twilio setup on convenience. The trade-off is a monthly fee that stays the same even if you make few calls.

How does your own Twilio number work on iPhone?

You rent a number directly from Twilio and pay per minute and per text. Twilio has no iPhone calling app of its own, so you decide how each direction works:

  • Incoming calls. Forward them to your iPhone using a TwiML Bin. They ring as ordinary calls on your regular number.
  • Outgoing calls. Use the API, or an app such as Dialbubble. Twilio rings your regular phone, then dials the other person, who sees your Twilio number. Dialbubble has no in-app calling, so two-way calls bill two legs.
  • Texts. US long-code numbers need A2P 10DLC registration. You read and send them through an app or the API. Dialbubble checks for new texts while it's open and has no push notifications, so urgent texts could wait.

If you already have a business line, you may not need to buy anything. You can verify that line as a caller ID at Twilio and show it on outgoing calls. It works for calls only, so you can't text from it through Twilio.

What this means

In a busy month, Twilio comes close to Quo's $15 Starter price, which also has a $1.50 to $3 messaging fee. Replies cost extra on Twilio ($0.0083 each plus any carrier fee), and registration adds a one-time $4.50 brand fee and $15 campaign fee. So at this level, the savings are small and a hosted app may be worth the difference. Twilio wins in quiet months, or if you skip texting: without the last two rows, the busy month falls to $9.00. Which second number should you choose?

  • Choose an eSIM if you want it to feel like a normal phone line and are happy to pay for a plan.
  • Choose a hosted app if you want to answer calls in an app, share a line with a team, or never think about setup. Dialbubble isn't the right choice if you need any of that.
  • Choose Google Voice if you're in the US, on a tight budget and your needs are simple.
  • Choose your own Twilio number if your use is low or uneven, you want several numbers, or you already use Twilio. There's more on how to use a Twilio phone number, and on showing an existing number in how to call from a different number.

Questions people ask

Can I have two phone numbers on one iPhone?
Yes. With Dual SIM, an iPhone XS, XR or later can have two active numbers using a physical SIM and an eSIM, or two eSIMs on iPhone 13 and later. Your carrier needs to support eSIM. Apps can also add numbers without a second plan.
What's the cheapest second phone number for a business?
Google Voice's basic plan has no cost where it's available. Among paid routes, a $1.15 a month Twilio number is cheap at low volume, though usage and text registration add to it. Compare your actual minutes and texts rather than the headline price.
Do I need a separate app to use a Twilio number on my iPhone?
To call out and text from your iPhone without using the API or the Twilio Console, yes. For incoming calls, no: forward them to your regular number at Twilio and they ring like any other call.
Will people see my personal number?
Not on calls placed through the business number. With a forwarded incoming call, the caller only sees the number they dialed. Calls you make directly from your personal line still show your personal number.
