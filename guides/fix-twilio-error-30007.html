Twilio error 30007: how to fix message filtered

To fix Twilio error 30007, first work out why your message was filtered: an unregistered sender, a forbidden use case, a suspicious link or spam-like wording. Then correct that cause and test slowly, and only ask Twilio Support to review once your messages comply.

This is the working-through guide. For a quick definition, see our reference page for error 30007.

What error 30007 is telling you

Twilio's definition is that your message was blocked by Twilio or by a carrier. Two things make it hard to fix: you aren't told which rule you broke, and filters can react to your overall traffic as well as to one text. Treat it as a process of elimination, and go through these steps in order. The early ones are the quickest to rule out.

Not legal advice

This guide covers compliance topics in general terms. It isn't legal advice. Ask a lawyer what the law requires for your own messages.

Look at one failed message rather than going by a report or a dashboard total:

Step 2: Check your sender is registered

Twilio says some carriers filter specifically to catch A2P traffic sent without registration, so check this before you touch any wording.

  • US local number: the number must be in the sender pool of a Messaging Service linked to an approved campaign. If you haven't done this, start with our A2P 10DLC registration guide.
  • Toll-free number: it needs approved verification.
  • A registered campaign: reread what you registered. If you now send marketing on a campaign that was approved for appointment reminders, your traffic no longer matches.

Step 3: Find the pattern

List your recent messages and compare the ones with 30007 against the ones that got through:

PatternPoints to
Every message fails, whatever the wordingRegistration or the sender (step 2)
Only messages with one link failThe link or its domain (step 5)
Only one template failsThe wording (step 5)
Failures began suddenly with no change from youA filter recently learned your pattern, or complaints rose (step 6)
Only a new or bought list failsConsent (step 6)

Twilio also notes that a sudden spike in 30007 suggests a filter has recently identified a pattern in your messages. Also keep in mind that carriers may rarely report a filtered message as delivered, so a few delivered statuses aren't proof that all is well.

Step 4: Check your use case is allowed

Twilio's forbidden message categories for the US and Canada include gambling, cannabis and CBD, phishing, third-party lead generation, debt relief, and high-risk loans. Firearms and vaping are barred on all number types, and alcohol needs proper age gating. If your use case falls in a forbidden category, no wording will save it, so you'll need to rethink SMS as a channel for it.

Step 5: Fix your links and rewrite the message

Links come first. Twilio's filtering advice warns against shared public shorteners such as free bit.ly links, because spammers use them too. Use a domain you own and that matches your business. Then look at the wording. Here is a blocked-style text, followed by a rewrite. Both come from a fictional shop:

BeforeAfterWhy it helps
No sender namedStarts with the business nameThe reader knows who it is
URGENT, WON, FREE, NOWNormal capitals, a real offer and dateTwilio warns against odd capitalization

There's a bonus on cost. The first version is 84 UCS-2 code units, so 2 segments, while the second is 135 GSM-7 characters, so 1. You can read why in GSM-7 vs UCS-2 encoding. Rewriting a text doesn't guarantee delivery, since filters aren't public, and it isn't a way to slip unwanted messages past them.

Step 6: Check consent and opt-outs

Twilio says high opt-out rates and complaints play a major role in carrier filtering. If people didn't ask for your texts, better wording won't last.

  • Only message people who agreed to hear from you. Remove any bought or scraped numbers.
  • Put opt-out wording in your first message using a standard word like STOP. Twilio warns that nonstandard wording such as "text 2 to opt out" gets filtered.
  • For US and Canadian traffic, Twilio says carriers expect opt-out language in every third message, or at least once a month for lighter senders.
  • Check in with long-term contacts at least every 18 months.

Step 7: Test slowly

  1. Pick a few people who agreed to receive texts, ideally on different carriers.
  2. Send only the fixed message, one at a time.
  3. Fetch each message's status as in step 1, then ask whether it arrived.
  4. If those pass, increase volume gradually rather than resending the whole list at once.

Don't forget the bill while you test. As of November 2026, an undelivered message avoids the $0.001 failed-message fee, but Twilio charges its normal fees when delivery was attempted. At $0.0083 per segment plus a carrier fee, resending a one-segment text to 1,000 T-Mobile customers costs 1,000 x $0.0128 = $12.80 each time, even if every copy is blocked.

Step 8: Escalate to Twilio Support

If you've done all of the above and still believe your messages comply, Twilio invites you to contact Support. Include:

  • Three or more Message SIDs with undelivered status and error 30007, from the past 7 days
  • What you send, who receives it, and how those people opted in
  • Your campaign or toll-free verification details
  • What you already changed, and when

What shouldn't you do?

  • Don't spread sends across extra numbers. Twilio says using several numbers to get past filtering, known as snowshoeing, is prohibited.
  • Don't tweak spelling to dodge filters. Swapping letters for symbols looks even more like spam, and it can force UCS-2.
  • Don't retry in a loop. The same text at the same filter will usually get the same result, and you pay each time.

Questions

Am I charged for a message that gets error 30007?
Usually, yes. A 30007 message normally ends as undelivered. That means no $0.001 failed-message fee, but Twilio charges its normal fees when delivery was attempted.
Which words get a text filtered?
There's no public list, and filters look at patterns as well as words. Twilio's advice is to avoid forbidden categories, public short links, unnecessary special characters and odd capitalization.
Will A2P 10DLC registration fix 30007?
It often helps, because Twilio says registering results in lower filtering. It won't fix a forbidden use case, a message that looks like spam, or a list of people who didn't agree to hear from you.
How long does a filter block last?
Twilio doesn't publish a duration. Change what triggered it and test again. If you've done that and your traffic is compliant, send Support your examples.
Is this guide legal advice?
No. It covers Twilio's and carriers' published rules in plain words. For what the law requires of your messages, talk to a qualified lawyer.
