Dialbubble vs Google Voice for business

If you're in the US and just want a simple second number, or your team is on Google Workspace, Google Voice is likely the better choice, and it's much easier to set up. Dialbubble suits you if you already use Twilio, or want what Twilio offers: spoken message calls, custom TwiML and several numbers with no per-user plan.

Choose Google Voice if

  • You're in the US and want a free number, and light use suits you.
  • Your team already pays for Google Workspace.
  • You need calls answered in the app, on iPhone, iPad or Android.
  • You want auto attendants and ring groups from a Standard plan.
  • You'd rather not manage a developer account and registration.

Choose Dialbubble if

  • You already have a Twilio account and numbers.
  • You want voice message calls, audio or custom TwiML from your phone.
  • You want scheduled texts and broadcast from your own numbers.
  • You need several numbers or a toll-free number on one person's phone.
  • You're outside the US and Twilio offers numbers where you need them.

Costs: free is hard to beat

If you're eligible and your needs are simple, Google Voice's free number costs less than any Twilio setup. On Twilio with Dialbubble, 100 call minutes and 100 sent texts come to about $7.28 a month, and 500 minutes and 300 texts to about $21.14. With about 100 texts, Voice Starter at $10 a month costs less from a little under 200 minutes.

Estimate from Twilio's US list prices, November 2026: one local number at $1.15 a month, Talk calls billed as two legs at $0.014 a minute each, texts sent at $0.0083 plus up to $0.005 in carrier fees, and a $2.00 monthly Sole Proprietor campaign. It leaves out incoming calls and texts, recording, taxes and the one-time $19.50 registration. Sources: voice, SMS, A2P 10DLC fees. Try your own numbers in the Twilio pricing calculator.

Looking for a Google Voice alternative?

It helps to know why. If you can't get a number because you're outside the US, or you've hit its single-number limit, Twilio might fit, and you can read how to use a Twilio number and what it costs. If you want a friendlier business service, Grasshopper and Quo are closer alternatives than we are.

Common questions

Is Google Voice free for business?
Google's free consumer number is aimed at individuals. For business features, Google sells plans from $10 per user a month, either added to Google Workspace or, for one US user with a Gmail address, as a standalone plan.
Can I get Google Voice outside the US?
The free consumer number is only for US residents, according to Google's community guide. Workspace plans are sold in more countries: Google says Starter is available in 14.
Can Google take back a free Google Voice number?
Yes. The community guide says a free number can be reclaimed after 90 days without an outgoing call or text, following a warning 30 days before removal. Incoming calls and texts don't count as use.
Does Google Voice offer toll-free numbers?
Google's help page says it doesn't offer 1-800 numbers. Twilio lists US toll-free numbers at $2.15 a month as of November 2026.
